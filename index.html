About

I am a Ph.D. candidate in Political Science at the Schar School of Policy and Government, George Mason University. My research sits at the intersection of domestic politics, institutional behavior, and immigration policy, with a focus on Japan and the broader Indo-Pacific region.

I am a mixed-methods researcher, combining survey experimental design, quantitative analysis, and qualitative field research — including extensive interview-based fieldwork conducted in Japanese across Tokyo, Gunma, and Fukuoka. My work examines how domestic political structures, public opinion, and party factionalism shape immigration policy outcomes in advanced democracies.

My research has been published in the International Migration Review, the Journal of Conflict Resolution, and PS: Political Science & Politics. I am also co-author of a book forthcoming from Cambridge University Press. I am currently an Associate at The Asia Group, where I work on Japanese domestic politics, energy policy, and U.S.–Japan relations.

Research & Publications

"Contested Spaces: Immigration and Domestic Politics in Japan"

Doctoral Dissertation  ·  Chair: Justin Gest  ·  Committee: Lucas Nunez, Naoru Koizumi

A three-paper dissertation examining the domestic political determinants of immigration policy in Japan, combining survey experimental, qualitative, and formal/empirical methods.

Paper 1 Survey experiment examining Japanese public opinion on immigration and fertility, and the factors shaping attitudes toward policy reform.
Paper 2 Qualitative field research based on elite interviews conducted in Japan, following up on the findings of Paper 1.
Paper 3 Institutional Stickiness: Voting behavior of former faction members in the Liberal Democratic Party.
